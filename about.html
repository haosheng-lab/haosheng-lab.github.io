Engineering + Biology + AI

Research

Living systems change through development, aging, and regeneration. We develop soft, adaptive bioelectronics that remain connected to living tissues as they change, enabling long-term measurements of electrical activity.

Rooted in bioelectronics, materials science, mechanics, and AI, our research brings together electrophysiology and molecular measurements to connect new tools with biological questions.

Map changing living tissues

How can electronic interfaces remain connected as living tissues change?

Living tissues change in mechanical properties and cellular organization. Electronic interfaces need to accommodate these changes to provide a consistent view of biological activity over time. This challenge connects materials, mechanics, and electrical engineering.

Our work on Cyborg Embryos and Spring electronics demonstrates how soft, adaptive interfaces can support recordings during brain development.

In the future, we aim to develop next-generation bioelectronic interfaces that adapt to living tissues and expand what can be measured across time and scale.

Understand cell dynamics

How do cellular electrical activity, identity, and tissue organization shape function over time?

Living tissues function through interactions among cells as they develop and change. In the nervous system, these interactions take the form of electrical and molecular communication among neurons within evolving circuits. Following neural activity over time helps connect changes in individual cells with the organization and function of neural networks.

Our work (AutoSort; FuseMap) brings together electrophysiology and molecular measurements, leveraging AI to interpret biological activity across time and scale.

In the future, we aim to uncover how neural activity and cellular identity relate to the emergence of circuit function and how it changes over time.

Guide precise modulation

What makes an intervention precise in a changing biological system?

The effect of a perturbation depends on the state of the cells and tissue in which it acts. Cellular activity, identity, and tissue organization shape that response. Precise modulation therefore requires an understanding of both the biological context and how stimulation is delivered.

Building on next-generation bioelectronics and a deeper understanding of changing living tissues, we aim to develop precise approaches to modulation. By combining measurement with intervention, we seek to track cellular responses over time and connect them with tissue function, guiding when, where, and how to interact with living systems.
